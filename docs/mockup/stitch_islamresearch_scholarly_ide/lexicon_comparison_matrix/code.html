Morphological Notes
Lane's Lexicon Vol 3, Pg. 1055

Mercy, pity, or compassion; tenderness of heart. Inclination requiring the exercise of favor and beneficence.

رَحِمَهُ // He had mercy on him
الرَّحْمَان // The Compassionate (denoting extensive mercy)

Form I verbal noun: رَحْمَةٌ or مَرْحَمَةٌ. When applied to God, it signifies the conferring of favor, abstracting the physical sense of 'tenderness' which is impossible for the Divine.

Lisan al-Arab Ibn Manzur

Softness (الرقة) and compassion (التعطف). Also refers fundamentally to the womb (الرحم) due to the innate connection and care it necessitates.

وَصَلَ رَحِمَهُ // He maintained ties of kinship

Highlights the etymological link between "mercy" (رحمة) and the "womb" (رحم), suggesting that mercy is a structural binding force among kin before being a generalized virtue.

Al-Mufradat al-Raghib

A gentleness requiring the exercise of beneficence toward the one shown mercy. It can be used for the feeling alone, or the resultant action alone.

Qur'an 7:156 وَرَحْمَتِي وَسِعَتْ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ

Differentiates usage: When humans show it, it entails emotional gentleness (رقة). When God shows it, it implies pure beneficence (إحسان) devoid of emotional vulnerability.

Taj al-Arus al-Zabidi

Synthesizes prior definitions. Focuses heavily on the derivational nouns and their exact metrical implications in poetry and theology.

مَرْحُوم // The object of mercy

Notes the intensive hyperbolic forms فَعْلان (Rahman) vs فَعِيل (Rahim) extensively, quoting early grammarians on the temporal vs permanent nature of the attributes.
